Functionally Graded Materials for Thermal Management

Senior capstone · GWU–NSWC Crane, with Lockheed Martin · 2026–27

A functionally graded material (FGM) changes composition gradually through its thickness, for example from a heat-resistant ceramic on the hot face to a tough metal on the cool face. With no sharp interface, there's no single bond line where thermal stress concentrates.

  • Designing FGM stacks to improve thermal management and lower thermal stress in aerospace and defense parts.
  • Comparing metal–ceramic and metal–metal pairs by thermal conductivity, thermal expansion, and Young's modulus using FEA in COMSOL and ABAQUS.
  • Built the analytical baseline first: a free tungsten bar heated to 1800 °C (9.35 mm growth over 1 m, zero stress), then Timoshenko's bimetal strip model checked with a Python script.

Toy Ferris Wheel Drivetrain

MAE 3191 · Mechanical Design · Team of 3

A toy Ferris wheel driven by a small DC motor. The motor spins at 2500 rpm with only 0.0565 N·m of torque, so most of the design is a large speed reduction.

  • Designed the power transmission: spur gears into a 40:1 worm and worm gear, then a timing belt to the wheel. The worm can't be back-driven, so the wheel holds position when the motor stops.
  • Ran the shaft analysis on a 3 mm 12L14 steel shaft: loads, shear and moment diagrams, von Mises stress. Factor of safety was 7.05.
  • Wrote and solved two homework problems on gear trains and shaft torque for the next year's class.

3D-Printed IPMC Soft Actuators

Research · ART-Med Lab · MAE Department

Ionic polymer-metal composites (IPMCs) are soft actuators that bend when a few volts are applied, because ions inside the polymer move toward one electrode. They're light, flexible, and quiet, which makes them a good fit for robots that work around people.

  • Compared two approaches: a low-cost, biocompatible, direct-ink-written membrane (124° bend, 0.82 cm⁻¹ curvature, 0.76 mN blocked force) and fused-filament printed IPMCs controlled with Bayesian optimization.
  • Proposed combining the two: biocompatible printed membranes with machine-learning control to recover force output.
  • In the lab, designed and fabricated an IPMC actuator: material processing, electrode deposition, and electromechanical displacement testing.
  • Soft robotics
  • 3D printing
  • Bayesian optimization

About me

an engineer, among other things

Why travel?

One might ask, "why put a travel blog in a portfolio? It has nothing to do with engineering." As an engineer, I'm always on the lookout for new and exciting challenges. That's why I have included a section in my portfolio dedicated to my travel adventures. Traveling is not only exhilarating but also a fantastic way to develop skills and traits that are essential for success in any field, especially engineering.

From hiking through remote mountain ranges to exploring bustling cities on my own, I have discovered that travel is a gateway to personal and professional growth. It requires problem-solving skills, adaptability, and an adventurous spirit, all of which are crucial qualities for engineers in this day and age. Not only does traveling push you outside of your comfort zone, but it also exposes you to different cultures and ways of thinking. It encourages an open mind and fosters creativity, which are essential qualities for engineers. It allows you to immerse yourself in new experiences, meet fascinating people, and learn about the world in ways that can't be replicated in a classroom or laboratory.

I believe that engineers who travel gain a unique perspective that enables them to tackle complex problems with more creativity and resourcefulness. These experiences can also help build self-confidence and resilience, both of which are essential in the fast-paced and often unpredictable field of engineering. So, I hope you enjoy the short stories & images of my travels and know that every experience I have encountered has fundamentally changed the way I look at the world and redefined my engineering pursuits.
